CHAPTER ONE INTRODUCTION
Background of the Study 
In today's rapidly evolving digital world, government institutions and public organizations are increasingly adopting computerized systems to improve efficiency, accountability, transparency, and public service delivery. One of the major challenges faced by many organizations and bureaus is the ineffective handling and management of complaints submitted by citizens and internal staff members. Complaints may arise due to poor service delivery, environmental issues, administrative delays, unethical practices, workplace conflicts, operational inefficiencies, or communication problems within the organization. Traditionally, complaint handling systems in many institutions are managed manually through paper-based documentation, physical office visits, telephone communication, or unstructured email exchanges. These traditional methods are often inefficient, time consuming, difficult to monitor, and vulnerable to errors, data loss, and poor accountability. Citizens frequently experience delays in receiving responses to their complaints, while staff members may lack a confidential and organized mechanism to report workplace-related concerns.
 The proposed Complaint Management System (CMS) is a web-based system designed to modernize and automate the complaint handling process within the bureau. The system will provide a centralized platform that enables both citizens and staff members to submit complaints electronically, upload supporting evidence, track complaint progress, receive notifications, and communicate with responsible officers. The system will also enable bureau staff and administrators to efficiently review, categorize, assign, investigate, monitor, and resolve complaints using a secure digital environment. Different categories of complaints such as environmental complaints, personal complaints, workplace complaints, administrative complaints, and service delivery complaints will be supported by the system. 
Furthermore, the system will provide role-based access control for different users including citizens, staff members, reviewers, department heads, and system administrators. Through automation and centralized data management, the bureau will improve complaint handling efficiency, reduce paperwork, strengthen transparency, enhance communication, and increase public trust in organizational services. The development of this Complaint Management System is therefore essential to support digital transformation initiatives, improve institutional responsiveness, and provide effective complaint management services for both external and internal users. 
Statement of the Problem
 Currently, many organizations and government bureaus manage complaints manually using notebooks, papers, spreadsheets, phone calls, or face-to-face communication. These traditional complaint handling methods create several operational challenges that negatively affect service quality, accountability, efficiency, and user satisfaction. Citizens often face difficulties when submitting complaints because they are required to physically visit offices, wait for long periods, or repeatedly follow up on complaint progress. In many cases, complaints are delayed, lost, ignored, or poorly documented due to the absence of a centralized complaint management platform. Similarly, staff members within the organization may experience workplace-related issues such as administrative conflicts, communication problems, unfair treatment, operational challenges, or service dissatisfaction. However, the organization may not have a secure and organized system that allows staff members to confidentially report and track such complaints. The major problems identified in the existing system include the following:
 1. Manual Complaint Registration Complaints are recorded manually, increasing the risk of errors, duplication, document damage, and loss of information. This process is time-consuming and highly vulnerable to human errors in data entry and documentation.
 2. Lack of Centralized Complaint Management There is no unified system that stores and manages complaints from both citizens and staff members in a centralized database. Complaint records are scattered across different departments, making comprehensive monitoring and management difficult. 
3. Poor Complaint Tracking Mechanism Complainants cannot easily monitor the status or progress of their complaints after submission. Once complaints are submitted, there is usually no transparent mechanism to track their current status or identify which department is handling them. 
4. Delayed Complaint Resolution Manual workflows slow down complaint assignment, investigation, review, and decision making processes. As a result, citizens and staff members experience long waiting times and dissatisfaction with service delivery.
 5. Lack of Transparency and Accountability It is difficult to monitor who handled complaints, what actions were taken, and whether complaints were resolved properly. The absence of proper tracking mechanisms reduces organizational accountability and transparency.
 6. Weak Communication Channels Communication between complainants and responsible officers is often inefficient and inconsistent. Without a centralized communication system, feedback mechanisms are poor and complainants may not receive timely updates. 
7. Difficulty in Managing Internal Staff Complaints The organization lacks a secure digital mechanism for handling workplace and staff related complaints confidentially. Staff members may hesitate to report internal issues due to concerns about privacy and confidentiality.
 8. Poor Reporting and Data Analysis Generating reports manually is time-consuming and makes it difficult for management to analyze complaint trends, department performance, resolution rates, and recurring organizational problems. This hinders effective decision-making and process improvement.
 9. Risk of Data Loss Paper documents and manual records can easily be misplaced, damaged, or lost, causing serious administrative and legal issues. Physical storage is vulnerable to environmental damage, theft, and unauthorized access.
 10. Limited Accessibility Citizens and staff members may face geographical and time limitations when submitting complaints physically. Transportation challenges, office hours, and distance barriers create inconvenience and reduce accessibility to complaint submission services. Due to these problems, there is a strong need for a computerized Complaint Management System that automates complaint submission, tracking, management, investigation, and resolution processes in an efficient, transparent, and secure manner.

6. 7. Technical Failures Problems such as server crashes, database failures, network interruptions, power outages, and software bugs may temporarily interrupt services and affect system availability. Proper backup and recovery mechanisms must be implemented.

CHAPTER TWO REQUIREMENT ANALYSIS AND DESIGN 
2.1 Overview of the Existing System 
The existing complaint handling system within many bureaus and public organizations is primarily manual and paper-based. Complaints submitted by citizens and staff members are commonly recorded using notebooks, paper forms, spreadsheets, telephone calls, or verbal communication. These traditional approaches are inefficient, time-consuming, difficult to monitor, and highly vulnerable to human error. In the current system, citizens who want to submit complaints are usually required to physically visit the bureau office and manually fill out complaint forms. Similarly, staff members who want to report workplace-related issues often rely on informal communication methods or manual documentation processes. Once complaints are submitted, the information is manually forwarded to responsible departments for review and investigation. The existing system lacks centralized data management and proper communication mechanisms between complainants and responsible officers. Complaint records are scattered across different offices and departments, making it difficult to track complaint progress, generate reports, and ensure accountability. Furthermore, the current manual system does not provide automated notifications, digital evidence management, complaint tracking capabilities, or centralized reporting tools. As a result, complaint resolution processes are often delayed, poorly monitored, and difficult to manage effectively. 
2.1.1 Key Features of the Existing System 
The current complaint handling system includes several traditional operational features. These features are discussed below. 
1. Manual Complaint Submission Citizens and staff members submit complaints physically using paper forms, written letters, or verbal communication methods. This process requires complainants to visit offices in person, wait for available officers, and complete manual documentation procedures. 
2. Paper-Based Documentation Complaint information and investigation records are stored in physical files and folders. These documents are maintained in filing cabinets, requiring physical storage space and manual organization systems. 
3. Manual Complaint Assignment Complaints are manually assigned to responsible departments or officers by administrators. This process involves reviewing complaint details, determining appropriate handling departments, and manually forwarding documents. 
4. Face-to-Face Communication Most communication between complainants and responsible officers occurs through physical office visits or telephone calls. This method is time-consuming and lacks proper documentation of communication history. 
5. Manual Investigation and Resolution Complaint investigations and decisions are performed manually without digital workflow automation. Investigators must physically access complaint files, document findings manually, and prepare written reports. 
6. Limited Complaint Tracking Complainants cannot monitor complaint progress online and must repeatedly contact offices for updates. This creates inconvenience and frustration for users seeking information about their complaints.
7. Manual Report Generation Reports and summaries are prepared manually using written documents or spreadsheets. This process is time-consuming and prone to errors in data compilation and analysis.
 8. Decentralized Record Management Complaint records are distributed across different offices and departments, making information retrieval difficult. Finding specific complaint records requires searching through multiple physical locations.
 2.1.2 Limitations of the Existing System
 The existing system faces several challenges that significantly reduce organizational efficiency and service quality: Delayed Complaint Handling Manual processing workflows create significant delays in complaint registration, assignment, investigation, and resolution. Complaints may remain unprocessed for extended periods due to bureaucratic procedures and manual document handling. Poor Transparency The absence of complaint tracking mechanisms means complainants cannot monitor the progress of their complaints. This lack of transparency creates mistrust and dissatisfaction among citizens and staff members. Weak Communication between complainants and responsible officers is inconsistent and poorly documented. Important information may be lost or misunderstood during verbal exchanges without proper record-keeping. High Paperwork Dependency The system relies heavily on physical documents, creating excessive paperwork that consumes storage space, requires manual organization, and is vulnerable to damage or loss. Risk of Document Loss Physical files can be misplaced, damaged by environmental factors, stolen, or destroyed. Loss of complaint records creates serious administrative and legal problems for the organization. Poor Accountability Without proper tracking and monitoring systems, it becomes difficult to determine responsibility for complaint handling delays or failures. This reduces organizational accountability and performance.
 Difficulty in Generating Reports Manual report preparation is time-consuming and error-prone. Management cannot easily access real-time complaint statistics, trends, or performance metrics for decision making. Lack of Centralized Data Management Complaint information is scattered across multiple departments and locations, making comprehensive data analysis and reporting nearly impossible. This fragmentation hinders organizational oversight and coordination. Limited Accessibility for Users Geographical barriers, office hours, and transportation requirements limit access to complaint submission services. Users must physically visit offices, creating inconvenience and reducing service accessibility. These limitations create a compelling need for a modern computerized Complaint Management System that can address these challenges through automation, centralization, and digital workflow management.

CHAPTER THREE SYSTEM ARCHITECTURE
 3.1 System Software Architecture
 The proposed Complaint Management System (CMS) is designed as a secure, scalable, and responsive web-based application. To ensure high modularity, ease of maintenance, and flexibility for future upgrades, the system follows a multi-tier software architecture. This separation of concerns allows developers to modify individual layers (such as changing the user interface design or optimizing database queries) without affecting other components of the system.
 3.1.1 Architectural Overview
 The CMS architecture is structured into four distinct logical layers, as described below: Presentation Layer (Client-Side) React.js SPA (Web Browser) Security Layer (Cross-Cutting) SSL/TLS Encryption & JWT verification Application Layer (Server-Side) Node.js / Express.js API Gateway Complaint Workflow Engine Notification Service Database Layer (Storage) PostgreSQL DBMS Secure File Repository (Evidence Uploads)
 1. Presentation Layer (Client-Side) The Presentation Layer represents the user interface (UI) through which users interact with the system. Built using React.js as a Single Page Application (SPA), this layer runs entirely within the user's web browser. It is responsible for rendering dashboards, forms, and tracking timelines dynamically based on user roles (Citizen, Staff, Officer, Department Head, Admin). It communicates with the Application Layer via asynchronous HTTP requests (AJAX/Fetch API). 
2. Application Layer (Server-Side) The Application Layer contains the core business logic, workflow rules, and server configurations of the CMS. Powered by Node.js and Express.js, this layer exposes a RESTful API to handle incoming client requests. It processes actions such as user authentication, complaint validation, automated routing to departments, deadline notifications, and report generation. 
3. Database Layer (Storage) The Database Layer handles persistent data storage, retrieval, and transactional integrity. It consists of: PostgreSQL Relational Database: Stores structured data including user credentials, user profiles, organizational structures, complaint records, status change history, assignment logs, and audit trails. Secure File Storage: A dedicated directory or cloud storage bucket utilized to store uploaded evidence attachments (documents, images, videos, audio recordings) linked to complaints. 
4. Security Layer (Cross-Cutting) The Security Layer spans across all tiers to protect system integrity and data confidentiality. It enforces SSL/TLS encryption for all data in transit, handles user session management using secure JSON Web Tokens (JWT), and verifies access permissions at the API endpoint level via Role-Based Access Control (RBAC).
 3.1.2 System Components 
The detailed specifications of the core components within the CMS architecture are outlined below:
 Component-to-Component Interactions
 1. User Request: A complainant submits a complaint form via the React.js client interface.
 2. Input Validation: The React client performs client-side validation (checks for empty fields, valid email format) and packages the form payload as a JSON object, along with any evidence attachments as form data. 
3. Transmission & Decryption: The request is transmitted over an encrypted HTTPS connection. The server decrypts the incoming request packet. 
4. Middleware Filtering: The Express.js router passes the request through security check Authentication Middleware: Verifies the signature and expiration date of the client's JWT. Access Control Middleware: Assesses whether the user's role possesses permissions to submit complaints. Input Sanitization Middleware: Cleans text descriptions to prevent cross-site scripting (XSS) and SQL injection vulnerabilities.
 5. Business Logic Execution: The server-side controller parses the sanitized data, generates a unique complaint reference number, saves file attachments to secure storage, and creates a database transaction to insert the complaint record. 
6. Data Persistence: PostgreSQL writes the transaction to persistent disk storage and updates indexes. 
7. Automated Notification: Once the database write is confirmed, the server invokes the Notification Service to dispatch: An email acknowledgment to the complainant containing the reference number. A system notification to the assigned department administrator. 
8.Response Render: The server sends a 201 Created HTTP response back to the client. The React client. The React client dynamically updates the UI to display the submission success screen and tracking ID.
